Research

My research focuses on process systems engineering, first-principles bioprocess modeling, dynamic simulation, process optimization, and sustainability assessment of waste-to-energy systems, advised by Dr. Selen Cremaschi at Auburn University.

  • First-Principles Bioprocess Modeling – Python-based dynamic models for anaerobic digestion involving nonlinear reaction kinetics, gas-liquid mass transfer, phase equilibria, ionic speciation, and pH-dependent inhibition, calibrated with nonlinear parameter estimation and constrained chi-squared minimization.
  • Process Simulation and Scale-Up – Aspen Plus and SuperPro Designer process models for gas upgrading, separation, and combined heat and power configurations, used to evaluate scale-sensitive design variables such as hydraulic retention time, organic loading rate, feed composition, and reactor volume.
  • Techno-Economic Analysis and Life Cycle Assessment – Integrated process simulation with techno-economic analysis and life cycle assessment to compare pulp and paper mill sludge valorization pathways based on performance, capital and operating cost, and environmental impact.
  • Microstructure-Informed Process Modeling – Representing pulp and paper mill sludge as a heterogeneous fibrous structure to quantify cellulose accessibility for hydrolysis modeling, and developing a multiscale NaOH pretreatment and anaerobic digestion framework linking accessibility changes to calibrated hydrolysis kinetics for improved methane production prediction.
  • Machine Learning for Biomanufacturing – CNN-based image analysis workflows for classifying cardiomyocyte differentiation outcomes from phase-contrast microscopy images, applying data preprocessing, model training, and performance evaluation.
