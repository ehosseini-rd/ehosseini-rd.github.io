Dynamic modeling of anaerobic digestion

How do heterogeneous feedstocks and operating conditions affect methane production and reactor stability?

I extend the Anaerobic Digestion Model No. 1 (ADM1) in Python to represent pulp and paper mill sludge and food waste. The framework combines biochemical kinetics, mass balances, gas–liquid transfer, physicochemical equilibria, and inhibition. Feedstock-specific parameters are estimated from published experimental data using bounded nonlinear least squares and multistart initialization.

~1,500feed-composition cases
15food-waste categories

Simulation workflows map methane productivity and feasible operating regions. Selected mixtures showed a model-predicted 17% higher maximum stable tested loading rate than the food-waste-only reference case. This is a simulation result within the tested conditions.

Techno-economic and life-cycle assessment

Can digestion strategies reduce the environmental burden of mill sludge, and at what cost?

My simulation-based screening study models a hypothetical kraft mill in the southeastern United States processing 500 tonnes of wet sludge per day (15 tonnes of total solids). It compares dewatering and landfilling with conventional digestion, NaOH pretreatment, and food-waste co-digestion, each under combined heat and power or biomethane production.

SuperPro Designer flowsheets support equipment sizing, capital and operating costs, and discounted cash-flow analysis. The LCA uses openLCA, ecoinvent 3.10, and nine TRACI 2.1 impact categories. Sensitivity analysis, 10,000 Monte Carlo draws, and scenario comparisons examine the influence of technical and market assumptions.

What the study shows

  • Co-digestion has the lowest cost per tonne of solids among the modeled sludge-digestion options.
  • All sludge-digestion options remain more costly than landfilling across all 10,000 Monte Carlo draws.
  • All digestion scenarios lower global warming impacts relative to sludge landfilling across the uncertainty draws.
  • The base-case co-digestion pathways have net climate impacts of −17.5 to −18.6 tonnes CO₂ equivalent per day, largely from avoided food-waste landfilling.

These are model-based screening results, conditional on the assumed feedstock composition, conversion efficiencies, prices, methane losses, and digestate management. Other environmental categories involve tradeoffs.

Superstructure modeling and optimization

Ongoing research

Which combinations of technologies offer the most promising routes for managing primary and secondary sludge from kraft pulp mills?

I develop a superstructure optimization framework in Python and Pyomo that represents alternative processing routes and their connections in a single mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) model. The framework considers biochemical conversion, thermochemical treatment, material recovery, and disposal, together with feed allocation, process selection, capacity, utility requirements, and residue management.

The model evaluates annual profit and net global warming potential. An epsilon-constraint approach traces the Pareto frontier to show the tradeoffs between economic and climate objectives. Scenario, sensitivity, and Monte Carlo analyses examine how feedstock properties, energy sources, market conditions, and technology assumptions affect the selected configuration.

The objective is to identify promising process networks and the conditions under which they become competitive, while accounting for integration with existing mill infrastructure.
